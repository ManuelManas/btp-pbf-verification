Conclusion
----------
I found no mismatch between the final relative theorem and the mathematical
meaning of Theorem 3.33. The formal development now covers both directions
of its criterion, positive factors with the prescribed initial entries,
real analytic dependence in the uniform relative norms, local Lipschitz
control, and GLOBAL uniqueness of the ordered positive factors with those
initial entries. The last assertion is proved separately by
relative_full_factors_global_unique; it is not inferred from local IFT
uniqueness.

The final theorem does not assume uniform absolute upper or lower bounds
on the base entries. It does not assume an inverse estimate or an analytic
LDU map as part of its final hypotheses. Intermediate bounds are proved
from the geometric conditions.

This is a statement about infinite matrices and relative entrywise norms.
It does not assert that every such unbounded matrix or factor defines a
closed operator on unweighted l2. That distinction agrees with the scope
of the manuscript's relative criterion.

Definitions and indices checked
-------------------------------
The base arrays a_i(n), b_j(n), d_n are arbitrary real functions, positive
on their genuine entries. They are not bounded-sequence objects. The
bounded-sequence objects describe RELATIVE changes, not the base arrays.

relativePerturbedFactor is exactly a_i(n)*(1+h_i(n)), with the finite
initial portion of h_i extended by zero. relativeProduct_actual and the
corresponding LDU/full-product identities identify these maps with actual
finite sums defining the matrix entries. The product maps are not merely
abstract surrogates of a matrix multiplication map.

Factor indices in Fin p are zero-based. The restriction n < p-i.val is
exactly 1 <= n <= p-i for the manuscript's one-based factor index i.
Row zero is not an actual subdiagonal entry. Requiring a positive value
there in the analytic constructions is a harmless extension: its
variation is zero and actual matrix coefficients do not use it. The
uniqueness core explicitly proves independence from these fictitious
row-zero entries.

The parameters p,q of the final full criterion occur as Fin(p+1),
Fin(q+1). Thus they cover every positive lower and upper bandwidth. A
side of bandwidth one has an empty adjacent-condition family.

GeometricDecay uses consecutive windows whose first row is s+1, s >= 0.
This is the manuscript's range n >= 1. The condition for length zero is
automatic. RelativeAdjacentGeometric uses same-row ratios; the crossed
weight is exactly
  a_last(n) * b_last(n) * d(n-1) / d(n).

Sufficiency: hypothesis audit
-----------------------------
I read RelativeLDUSufficiency and its estimates in RelativeActualControl,
RelativeNormalizedControl, RelativeFiniteControl, RelativeFiniteEstimates,
RelativeLDUScaling and RelativeLDUInverse.

RelativeLDUData encodes actual finite triangular sections, their inverse
identities, coherence, and identification with the raw matrix entries.
It does not encode the analytic conclusion or a uniform inverse bound.
factorRelativeData constructs it from the original arrays and positive
diagonal without an absolute boundedness assumption.

The normalization divides all lower factor entries in a row by that
row's last lower factor, and independently does the analogous operation
on the upper side. RelativeLDUProduct_scale proves exact invariance of
the relative nonlinear product under the corresponding two-sided
scaling. This does NOT reduce the argument to the old theorem with
uniformly positive bounded factors: normalized smaller factors may tend
to zero, and the scaled diagonal may remain unbounded. The proof uses
new relative estimates for actual finite inverses.

normalized_finite_control derives constants independent of the section
size from the adjacent and crossed geometric hypotheses. Its prefix
comparisons, inverse-entry bounds and weighted diagonal sums are proved,
not retained as assumptions of the final statement.

The lower derivative estimate uses the exact cancellation identity
  L*lowerPart(V) = E*U^{-1} - L*(diagPart(V)+upperPart(V)),
  V = L^{-1}*E*U^{-1}.
Consequently it retains the scale of the target entry L_ij; it does not
replace a tiny target coefficient by an absolute estimate. The upper
estimate is its transposed counterpart. Positivity assumptions used in
summing these estimates are present and discharged. These are the
critical bounds permitting arbitrary relative growth.

Finite inverse solutions are shown coherent and then define a bounded
linear map on all relative coefficient sequences. inverseMap_right
proves the inverse equation entry by entry using actual finite sections.
derivative_injective is obtained independently from finite triangular
uniqueness. The analytic IFT is then applied to the genuine polynomial
relative product map.

Necessity: hypothesis audit
--------------------------
The choice gives a bounded right inverse of its actual derivative by
the chain rule. Triangular extraction and normalized LDU injectivity
recover the two adjacent criteria without assuming an analytic LDU
selection in advance.

For the crossed condition, the forcing direction is the diagonal D
expressed relatively to T0, namely d_n/(T0)_nn. Its entries lie in [0,1].
The diagonal component of the formal inverse derivative is exactly the
weighted joint row sum of the two checkerboard-signed triangular
inverses. The bounded derivative therefore bounds these row sums. The
positive recurrence criterion then gives the crossed geometric decay.
No absolute boundedness of a,b,d is used in this argument.

Local inverse, Lipschitz and global uniqueness
--------------------------------------------
The local-inverse result has both identities, not only a right section.
The same relative sup norm controls every entry of every factor at once.
The Lipschitz conclusion and g(0)=0 give the manuscript's displayed
factor-error estimate after shrinking to a neighborhood containing zero.
The relative margins ensure positivity without an absolute alpha > 0.

Global uniqueness is separate. RelativeUniquenessCore proves uniqueness
of ordered triangular factors by strong induction on the row. Known
initial entries and the previous rows reduce the remaining row entries
to the matrix system of Lemma24, whose determinant is positive. Reversal
of factor order has been checked explicitly: the manuscript's initial
triangle becomes n <= j in the legacy right-to-left ordering.

RelativeGlobalUniqueness then uses the actual equality of finite LDU
sections to recover the diagonal and the two unit triangular products,
and applies the row-system uniqueness to each side. It covers all
positive factorizations with the prescribed initial entries, even ones
far from the base factorization. In fact positivity is only required
for the first factorization, since its row matrices determine the
linear systems. This stronger formulation is valid.

The core/global uniqueness files were authored by this reviewer, and
were independently read by the lean_audit_existing agent, who confirmed
the row system, initial-index reversal, and the stronger one-sided
positivity assumption. The sufficiency and final analytic assembly were
reviewed independently from their authors.

Actual operator norm for the bounded criteria
--------------------------------------------
BandOperatorNorm constructs continuous linear maps on
  lp (fun _ : Nat => Real) 2,
not on a formal coefficient proxy. Coordinate vectors recover the
matrix entries. The following estimates are proved:
  coefficient norm <= operator norm
  operator norm <= (p+q+1) * coefficient norm.

The range is characterized as ALL bounded real l2 operators with entries
zero outside the specified band. bandOperatorEquivRange is a continuous
linear equivalence with precisely that operator-norm subspace.
OperatorStability transports analytic choices, products, positivity and
initial-data conditions through it. Both fixed and free full-band
criteria have explicit operator-norm exports. The diagonal's upper
boundedness is encoded by d : Seq; the other stated uniform assumptions
are compatible with the manuscript.

The formal operator space is real l2. This suffices for the real matrix
perturbations in the manuscript. This report does not claim a separately
formalized complexification theorem or an operator-domain realization
for arbitrary-growth relative matrices.
