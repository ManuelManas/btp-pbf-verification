2. FullFixedEquivalence.lean
   full_fixed_positive_factorization_iff
   Corresponds to stab:thm:general-full-stability.
   For every positive lower and upper width (encoded p+1,q+1), under
   uniform positive lower and finite upper bounds for the prescribed
   bidiagonal sequences, analytic positive fixed-initial-entry choice is
   equivalent to the two manuscript adjacent conditions and the crossed
   condition on a_p b_q. The diagonal is a bounded sequence bounded away
   from zero. Sufficiency uses genuine inverse-product domination, not
   an assumed inverse or LDU choice. Necessity cancels the injective
   formal LDU derivative and derives the crossed condition from actual
   inverse row sums.

6. BandOperatorNorm.lean and OperatorStability.lean
   coefficientNorm_le_operatorNorm, norm_bandOperator_le
   bandOperator_basis, bandOperator_range_iff, bandOperatorEquivRange
   full_fixed_operator_criterion, full_free_operator_criterion
   The actual bounded operator on real ell^2 is constructed using shifts
   and multiplication operators. Its matrix entries equal the original
   band coefficients. The exact inequalities are
      coefficient norm <= operator norm <= (p+q+1) coefficient norm.
   The range is precisely the operators with the prescribed finite band.
   The analytic fixed/free criteria are transported through the proved
   continuous linear equivalence to operator-norm neighborhoods. The
   former coefficient/operator-norm scope gap has therefore been closed.

7. RelativeFullCriterion.lean
   relative_full_positive_factorization_iff
   relative_full_positive_factorization_with_lipschitz
   Corresponds to the equivalence and relative Lipschitz conclusion in
   stab:thm:relative-criterion, for arbitrary positive widths p+1,q+1.
   There are NO bounds on the prescribed positive factor entries or on
   the diagonal. The final hypotheses are exactly the same-row adjacent
   geometric conditions and the crossed condition
      G(a_last(n)*b_last(n)*d(n-1)/d(n)).
   The genuine normalized matrix product is used throughout. Its input
   and output coordinates are bounded RELATIVE variations, rather than
   bounded base entries. The proof derives finite inverse bounds, the
   bounded inverse and analytic section. These are not hypotheses of the
   final theorem. The entrywise relative topology is not being presented
   as a closed-operator or Kato perturbation theorem.

8. RelativeFullLocalCriterion.lean
   relative_full_local_inverse_of_geometric
   Specializes the actual finite-section factory factorRelativeData.
   Gives both local inverse identities, fixed initial entries, local
   Lipschitz dependence, and factor/diagonal lower bounds equal to half
   their positive base entries. No auxiliary finite inverse data or
   Bounds assumptions remain in its final statement.
   This result proves local uniqueness in relative factor norm.

9. RelativeGlobalUniqueness.lean
   relative_full_factors_global_unique
   Completes the global uniqueness conclusion of the relative theorem:
   positive physical factors, their full product and the prescribed
   initial entries determine the diagonal and every factor entry.
   No norm, growth, geometric-decay or closeness hypothesis is required.
   The proof uses genuine normalized LDU uniqueness, the exact factor-
   order bridge, and the invertible row systems of the audited Lemma 2.4.
   Independent reading verified the reversal of the initial-entry indices
   and the scope of the row induction. The theorem only needs positivity
   of the first factorization; the second solves the same uniquely
   determined linear row equations.

Relative semantic checks:
- rawReverse is proved to convert between the left-to-right factor list
  and the legacy right-to-left finite matrix product, for arbitrary raw
  sequences. No bound or convergence is needed for this algebraic bridge.
- Normalization by the cumulative product of each last factor is exact
  and preserves the normalized relative product. It is not an assumption
  that an arbitrary matrix is bounded after a diagonal change of scale.
- The normalized lower entries may approach zero. The lower/upper inverse
  estimates therefore preserve their relative scale by a genuine finite
  cancellation identity; an absolute coefficient bound alone is not used
  to assert a relative inverse bound.
- The scaled diagonal can remain unbounded. Only its backward weighted
  row sums, derived from G(cross), enter the inverse estimates.
- The final positivity assumption includes fictitious row-zero factor
  values. Those values are absent from all matrix entries and can be set
  arbitrarily positive; all physical subdiagonals begin at row one.
- Width one has no adjacent condition, as in the manuscript.
