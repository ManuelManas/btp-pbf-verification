Semantic review of the analytic general-stability formalization
Reviewer: analytic subagent. Date: 2026-10-19.

1. Spaces and physical matrix entries

Seq is the Banach space of bounded real sequences, implemented as bounded
continuous functions on the discrete space of natural numbers. LowerBand p
is Fin p -> Seq. Coordinate E(r,j) is the actual strict-lower matrix entry
(j+r+1,j). FullBand p q is LowerBand p x (Seq x LowerBand q); its last
component represents the transpose of the upper triangular factor.
These are genuine infinite sequence spaces, not finite truncations.

The row-zero value of a factor sequence is a dummy: a unit lower bidiagonal
has subdiagonal entries only in rows n>=1. All hypotheses use a positive
dummy extension at zero. This adds no condition on a physical matrix.

Fixed variations in factor i (zero-based indexing) begin at row p-i.
They therefore fix exactly p(p-1)/2 physical initial entries. Free
variations begin at row one in every factor. Their coordinate zero is the
variation of row one, and is not lost.

2. Actual products and derivatives

AnalyticMatrixRealization identifies lowerProductBand with ordinary
semi-infinite unit bidiagonal matrix multiplication. The factors in this
API are listed from left to right. AnalyticLDUMatrix proves that every
finite leading section of lduProduct is the ordinary normalized LDU
product. AnalyticLDUDerivativeMatrix proves the corresponding identity
for the explicitly computed Frechet derivative. No identification of a
postulated linear map with the matrix derivative is left as a hypothesis.

The legacy finite lowerProduct uses the opposite index order. That fact
must be addressed with reversedEntries when applying the legacy inverse
entry formula. FullFixedEquivalence's JointInverseRowsBounded does so.
In particular, the fixed-data cross weight is the product of the last
factors in the actual left-to-right lower and transposed-upper lists.

3. Complete lower-band fixed-data theorem

AnalyticLowerStability.fixed_initial_positive_factorization_iff_manuscript
is an actual arbitrary-p equivalence. Its assumptions are bounded real
factor sequences and a common positive lower bound. It concludes existence
of a positive analytic local factor choice through the prescribed factors,
with the specified initial entries fixed, exactly when adjacent geometric
window-product decay holds. The polynomial multiplication, its derivative,
the rowwise injectivity and the scalar inverse criterion are proved.
The existence of an inverse derivative is not assumed in this final result.
fixed_positive_choice_with_lipschitz proves the additional local Lipschitz
conclusion for the same choice.

4. Complete lower-band variable-data theorem

AnalyticFreeLowerStability proves both analytic-choice and
Frechet-differentiable-choice equivalences with all pair conditions.
The necessity is not inferred from an injectivity assumption: the actual
surjective derivative induces the genuine componentwise quotient by c0;
successive extraction and the scalar quotient criterion force every pair.
Sufficiency constructs bounded right inverses from the scalar alternatives
and applies a proved Banach analytic local-section theorem.

ManuscriptFreePairCondition uses a_i(n)/a_j(n-(j-i)), with n>=j-i+1.
The subtraction is deliberately grouped, since natural-number subtraction
n-j+i would be incorrect when n<j. geometric_reindex_from_row proves the
equivalence to the extraction coordinates. The geometric direction can
differ by pair. No assumption that all pairs decay in the same direction
has slipped into the final theorem. Local Lipschitz is also proved.

5. Normalized LDU and full-band assembly

ActualAnalyticLDU removes the intermediate IsDerivative hypothesis by the
proved matrix-derivative identity. The row criterion is stated for positive
lower and upper bandwidths p+1,q+1; the pure triangular cases have their
separate lower-band results. A diagonal bounded away from zero is required.

FullFixedDerivative and FullFreeTransfer differentiate the actual full
product through its triangular products and normalized LDU. They also
prove that a full analytic choice implies an analytic normalized LDU
choice and a bounded right inverse of the actual full derivative.
These implications do not assume a joint-row bound.

Independent follow-up review: FullFixedEquivalence and FullFreeCriterion

The b list represents U transpose from left to right, so the actual upper

Both final equivalences compiled strictly. The package-wide fresh source

6. Precisely delimited scope

The product maps first use the supremum norm on the finite family of band
coefficient sequences and on the factors. BandOperatorNorm now constructs
the actual operators on l2, proves coefficient norm <= operator norm <=
(p+q+1) coefficient norm, and identifies their range with all bounded
operators having the prescribed band. OperatorStability transports the
fixed and free analytic criteria to that operator-norm space. Thus this
norm bridge is itself formalized, rather than merely asserted in prose.

The arbitrary-growth relative full-band criterion has now also been
formalized (section 8 below). Interiority implies analytic LDU and the
local-family dimension theorem are not covered by this analytic audit.
Those results remain outside the current formal coverage. No claim about
the Toeplitz spectral theory follows
from the analytic stability modules.

8. Independent audit of the arbitrary-growth product maps

RelativeProduct and RelativeLDUProduct have been read independently.
The base coefficients are positive raw functions of the row, without a
uniform upper bound or a positive uniform lower bound. The bounded
variables are relative perturbations, not the original coefficients.
Each polynomial path is divided by the corresponding actual base matrix
entry. Positivity makes every normalized path contribution at most one.
The actual-entry identities prove that the output is precisely Delta T/T,
so no different normalization is silently substituted in the analytic map.

In the LDU formulas, j is the minimum of the row and column indices and
t=j-k parametrizes the summation index k. The lower formula includes all
terms with t<=q, the upper formula all terms with t<=p, and the diagonal
formula all terms with t<=q. The remaining terms vanish by bandwidth.
The denominator is strictly positive in every output coordinate.
The differential formula is the actual normalized sum of delta L D U,
L delta D U and L D delta U. No inverse or stability condition appears
as an assumption in these product/derivative identifications.

RelativePositiveFactors now proves the analytic local-section mechanism,
positivity, fixed initial entries and local Lipschitz dependence for this
actual relative product. RelativeProductInduction proves the exact
derivative decomposition into left extraction and the remaining product.
Both compiled with warnings as errors and the standard three axioms only.
RelativeLowerCriterion now proves the complete arbitrary-bandwidth lower
equivalence: a positive analytic choice in relative norms with fixed
initial entries exists if and only if all adjacent same-row weights have
uniform geometric products. It also gives the local Lipschitz conclusion.
Its proof combines the actual extraction derivative with the proved
forcing/remainder estimates, rowwise injectivity and necessity obtained
from actual surjectivity. No comparison constant K, positive uniform lower
bound, inverse derivative or desired conclusion is left as a hypothesis.
RelativeLDUInverse constructs the continuous inverse from compatible
finite triangular solves and actual relative bounds. Its auxiliary
RelativeLDUData stores algebraic finite inverses only, and Bounds stores
estimates subsequently proved by the full-band assembly. The proof of
derivative_injective does not assume Bounds: it follows from uniqueness
of finite triangular variations. RelativeLDUSufficiency obtains those
bounds from geometric products, proves invariance of the relative map
under diagonal normalization, and applies the actual analytic inverse.
RelativeLDUNecessity uses the actual diagonal forcing E=D and the chain
rule to force the crossed geometric condition. RelativeFullCriterion
then proves the full equivalence for arbitrary positive raw coefficients.
Its final statement contains only positivity and the explicit adjacent
and crossed products; no comparison constant, boundedness, algebraic data,
analytic section, or inverse estimate is left as an assumed conclusion.
The relative norm is the supremum of entrywise relative errors. This audit
does not turn it into a Kato relative operator norm or establish domains
and closedness for the unbounded matrices.

9. Uniqueness and the local inverse

AnalyticLowerLocalInverse and RelativeLowerLocalInverse additionally prove
both inverse identities in neighborhoods of the base matrix and factors,
not merely a right inverse. They preserve the same fixed initial entries,
give uniform absolute or relative positive margins, and establish local
Lipschitz dependence for that same inverse. FullFixedLocalInverse provides
the bounded full-band counterpart. The relative full-band counterpart is
in RelativeFullLocalInverse, with only the algebraic finite data needed
for injectivity. RelativeFullLocalCriterion instantiates those data from
the original factors and yields both identities from the geometric
conditions alone. All three new local-inverse exports have compiled with
warnings as errors and only the three standard axioms. These statements
give uniqueness among nearby factor sequences in the norm specified.

The additional RelativeGlobalUniqueness module now supplies the separate
nonlinear argument. Its relative_full_factors_global_unique theorem starts
from equality of the actual products and equality of the prescribed
physical initial entries; positivity of the original factors and diagonal
is sufficient. It concludes equality of the complete diagonals and of
every physical factor entry in both lists. There is no proximity, growth,
boundedness or geometric-decay assumption. I checked the ordering bridge
and the reduction to normalized LDU uniqueness and the rowwise positive
factor uniqueness theorem; the dummy row zero is correctly excluded.
