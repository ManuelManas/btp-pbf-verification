RELATIVE FULL-BAND CRITERION: SEMANTIC SCOPE
2026-10-19

Primary entry point:
  RelativeFullCriterion.lean
  BTP.RelativeStability.relative_full_positive_factorization_iff

The theorem is an equivalence, not only a sufficient condition. It applies
in every pair of positive finite bandwidths (represented by p+1,q+1).
The base lower/upper bidiagonal entries and diagonal entries are arbitrary
positive real sequences. They need not be bounded, bounded away from zero,
periodic, convergent, or comparable to a common growth rate.

The right side of the equivalence consists precisely of:
  G(a_i(n)/a_(i+1)(n)), for each adjacent lower pair;
  G(b_j(n)/b_(j+1)(n)), for each adjacent upper pair;
  G(a_last(n)*b_last(n)*d(n-1)/d(n)).
Here G bounds every consecutive finite product uniformly geometrically,
with constants independent of its initial row and length.

The left side is the existence of an actual analytic local factor choice:
  * its product equals every sufficiently small relative band perturbation;
  * its value at zero perturbation is exactly the given factorization;
  * all physical bidiagonal and diagonal entries remain positive;
  * the prescribed initial entries remain fixed.
The Banach coordinates are relative changes. A bounded coordinate array
E represents the physical matrix perturbation T0_ij*E_ij. Its norm is the
supremum of the relative entry changes. No assertion about a common closed
operator domain or Kato perturbations is being substituted for this norm.

Additional compiled conclusion:
  relative_full_positive_factorization_with_lipschitz
provides one analytic map with a uniform local Lipschitz bound for all
relative factor and diagonal changes simultaneously.

No inverse or matrix criterion is hidden among the final hypotheses.
The dependency chain contains the following proved links:
  1. The raw product coefficients equal finite products of bidiagonals in
     the stated order, and the upper-side convention is the transpose.
  2. The normalized LDU product is the genuine matrix product, and its
     derivative equals the genuine finite variation XDU+LZU+LDY.
  3. Finite triangular inverses give compatible variations of the correct
     bandwidth. Their relative bounds follow from explicit positive-path,
     inverse-product and cancellation estimates.
  4. Those estimates construct the bounded inverse in the relative Banach
     space; the analytic inverse-function theorem then supplies the map.
  5. Conversely, differentiating an analytic factor choice gives a bounded
     right inverse. Finite LDU injectivity and the complete relative lower
     criterion force both adjacent conditions. The D forcing direction
     forces the crossed condition through the true inverse row sums.

The sufficiency proof uses an exact normalization by cumulative products
of the last factors. It does not require the normalized triangular entries
to be bounded away from zero, or the normalized diagonal to be bounded.
The relative estimates preserve the scale of each individual matrix entry.
